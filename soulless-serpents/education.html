01 — Husbandry

Setting up for a snake that eats

Ball pythons are shy, nocturnal ambush hunters. Give them a temperature gradient, a snug hide and quiet, and almost everything else follows.

Heat is the single biggest factor. Provide a warm spot and a cool retreat so the snake can manage its own digestion, and control the heat source with a thermostat — not a dial or a guess. Measure with a digital probe or a temperature gun at the surface the snake actually sits on.

Feeding response depends on security more than food size. A hatchling in an oversized glass tank with one hide and a heat lamp overhead often refuses for weeks; the same snake in a small tub with two hides and belly heat typically eats the same night.

Five things that stop a ball python eating

  • Cool or unstable temperatures.
  • Too much open space, or only one hide.
  • Handling in the days before a feed.
  • Prey too large, too small, or offered too often.
  • Winter photoperiod, breeding season, or a pending shed.
Hot spot
88–90 °F (31–32 °C) on the surface, run through a thermostat
Cool side
78–80 °F (26–27 °C)
Ambient
Around 80 °F, stable day and night. No coloured bulbs needed
Humidity
55–60%, raised to 70–80% while in shed
Hatchling enclosure
24 × 18 × 12 in tub or vivarium — small is easier to control
Adult enclosure
4 × 2 × 2 ft minimum, with clutter and two hides
Substrate
Coco coir or cypress mulch that holds humidity without staying wet
Water
Fresh daily, bowl large enough to soak in
Feeding
Hatchling every 5–7 days, juvenile every 7, adult every 10–14
Quarantine
60–90 days in a separate room before any contact with the collection

02 — Genetics

What a morph actually is

A morph is just a gene variant that changes colour or pattern. The words around it — dominant, co-dominant, recessive, het — describe how it is passed on.

Every ball python carries two copies of each gene, one from each parent. How many copies it takes to see the effect is what the labels describe.

Hets, plainly

A recessive animal needs two copies to look like the morph. Carrying only one makes it a het — visually normal, and able to pass the gene to half its offspring.

Poss hets are the odds of an animal from a het breeding being a carrier. A 66% het is not a guarantee; it is a probability, and it should be priced like one.

Complexes, and why they matter

Some co-dominant genes share a locus — a spot on the same chromosome — so they interact instead of stacking. That is why Lesser and Mojave together produce a blue-eyed leucistic, and why Asphalt and Yellow Belly can produce a super stripe.

Two genes from the same complex in one animal produce something new, not a doubled-up version. Breeding within a complex is a decision to make on purpose.

Pairings that do not survive

Two alleles on the spider locus can produce eggs that never hatch. These crosses are never made, whatever the odds look like on paper.

  • Spider × Spider
  • Spider × Champagne
  • Champagne × Champagne
  • Woma × Woma
  • Hidden Gene Woma × Woma
  • Hidden Gene Woma × Hidden Gene Woma

Pairings that produce severe wobble

These hatch, but the wobble is severe rather than mild — a snake that may never feed or strike reliably. The pairing is disclosed before it is made, not after.

05 — FAQ

Questions we get asked

Do you ship, or is it pickup only?

Both. Courier shipping runs Monday to Wednesday with a live-arrival guarantee, and local pickup is by appointment. Shipping is paused in extreme heat or cold rather than risking the animal.

What are the animals eating?

Frozen/thawed rats, sized to the snake. Each listing carries its current prey size and feeding interval. Live feeders are available on request but we do not send animals out on live-only diets if it can be avoided.

Do you offer payment plans?

Yes — 25% down, balance within 30 days, animal marked as reserved for the duration. The deposit is non-refundable if the plan is abandoned, and shipping is scheduled once the balance clears.

Do you sell spider-complex animals?

Occasionally, always with the wobble disclosed and never as a visual-to-visual pairing. Champagne and spider are bred deliberately and sparingly, and affected animals are described honestly rather than quietly moved on.

It has not eaten since arriving. What now?

Leave it completely alone for seven days with correct temperatures and a hide, then offer a smaller prey item in the evening. Send a message before you panic — most refusals are temperature or settling, not illness.

Where do the records come from?

The Snake Room, our own breeding software. Weights, feeds, sheds, pairings, clutch data and hatch dates are logged as they happen, and the available list on this site is published from those records.

Still deciding?

Ask the awkward questions. A breeder who cannot answer them is a breeder worth avoiding.
